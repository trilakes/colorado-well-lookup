Durango well depths depend on the local ground

Durango-area wells have a 125-foot median depth. The middle half runs from 80 to 200 feet, so a driller bidding a typical parcel may be working within that range, while still allowing for a deeper hole. The 90th percentile is 340 feet, and the deepest record reaches 2,403 feet.

Depth records are mixed: 34% are under 100 feet, 51% fall from 100 to 300 feet, and 13% are between 300 and 600 feet. Permits most often name unnamed or fractured-bedrock aquifer conditions. In fractured rock, a short distance between lots can produce very different drilling results, so one nearby depth should not be treated as a guaranteed target.

How much water a Durango well may produce

The reported yield median is 12 gallons per minute, with the middle half from 7 to 15 gallons per minute. The yield mix is encouraging for many household uses: 46% of reported wells produce between 5 and 15 gallons per minute, while 42% report more than 15 gallons per minute.

Low-producing wells still occur. 11% report 1 to 5 gallons per minute, and 1% report under 1 gallon per minute. Only 337 wells in the records report a pump yield, compared with 2,498 total permitted wells, so these figures describe reported results rather than every well. Ask for a pumping test and storage plan, especially if the home will have irrigation, livestock, or several occupants.

Durango water levels and the recent record

Among the 73 wells reporting a static water level, the median depth to water is 58 feet, with the middle half from 32 to 80 feet below the surface. That gives a useful starting point for pump planning, but static levels vary with location, season, recent pumping, and the aquifer reached by the well.

The decade figures do not show a simple dropping pattern in this data. The median measured level was 70 feet below surface in the 2010s and 55 feet in the 2020s. Because the water-level sample is limited and the wells may not be comparable, do not use that change alone to predict long-term supply. A site-specific driller's evaluation and a properly documented water-level measurement matter more.

A realistic Durango well drilling budget

For the mixed sedimentary and alluvial ground described in the local cost model, a typical drilling rate is $50 to $75 per foot. At the 125-foot median depth, the drilled hole works out to about $6,200 to $9,400.

That is not the complete water-system price. A finished well including the pump, pressure tank, trenching, and electrical work is estimated at $12,200 to $21,400 at the median depth. A bid should state what happens if the hole must go beyond the planned depth, if casing or grout requirements change, or if the well needs additional development. Driveway access, power distance, terrain, and water treatment can also affect the final scope, so compare written bids line by line.

Risks to check before buying near Durango

Low yield and dry-hole risk deserve attention because fractured-bedrock conditions can change sharply across short distances. The local records include 1% of reported yields under 1 gallon per minute, and the deepest well is 2,403 feet. A permit map can identify nearby wells, but it cannot promise the same depth or production on your parcel.

Water quality also needs its own investigation. Within about 10 miles are 84 PFAS sampling or contamination sites, 41 historic mine features, and 12 EPA Superfund or cleanup sites. These mapped sites do not prove a problem at a particular house. Test an existing well before purchase and plan appropriate testing for a new well, including concerns tied to local land use and mine history. Confirm septic setbacks, building envelopes, access for a rig, and the permitted household use before relying on a property.

Buying or building a Durango-area lot

Start with the Colorado DWR permit record for the parcel and nearby wells, then compare depths, reported yields, water levels, use types, and aquifer descriptions. The surrounding record is mostly domestic or household use at 67%, with monitoring at 14%. That mix helps explain the local data but does not replace a review of the specific permit and legal water use.

Expand the search beyond Durango when useful. Nearby reference areas include Hesperus, Bayfield, Ignacio, Marvel, Southern Ute, and Mancos. Before closing, obtain a driller's access and depth opinion, price the full pump and electrical system, confirm septic and building setbacks, and check whether an augmentation plan is needed for the intended use. A pre-purchase water test and a written well contingency can prevent an expensive surprise.
