Montezuma well depth depends on fractured mountain rock

The recorded median well depth near Montezuma is 74 feet. The middle half runs from 45 to 141 feet, and 61% of reported wells are under 100 feet. Another 32% fall between 100 and 300 feet. The 90th percentile is 232 feet, while the deepest recorded well reaches 3,500 feet.

That spread matters when comparing drilling bids. A 74-foot estimate may fit the local midpoint, but a contractor still has to allow for a deeper hole if the first fractures do not produce water. The records name an unnamed or fractured-bedrock aquifer for 336 wells, with no Denver Basin aquifer wells. Ask for a written per-foot rate, casing and grout assumptions, and the point at which you can stop or revise the work.

What Montezuma wells typically produce

Among the 59 wells with a reported pump yield, the median is 10.0 gallons per minute. The middle half produces 8.0 to 15.0 gallons per minute. No reported wells are under 1 gallon per minute; 7% produce 1 to 5 gallons per minute, 59% produce 5 to 15 gallons per minute, and 34% produce more than 15 gallons per minute.

Those figures are encouraging for ordinary household use, but they are not a promise for an untested lot. Yield is different from storage in the well, and a strong short test does not answer every seasonal question. Have the driller document the pumping test, recovery, static water level, and proposed pump capacity before sizing a home system.

The water-level records are too limited for a trend

The 11 wells with static water-level records show a median depth to water of 40 feet, with the middle half between 25 and 58 feet. That gives a useful local reference for pump setting and trench planning, but the sample is small compared with the 337 permitted wells.

Permit activity by decade does not show the water table rising or falling. It shows 5 permits in the 1960s, 22 in the 1970s, 40 in the 1980s, 66 in the 1990s, 76 in the 2000s, 29 in the 2010s, and 10 in the 2020s. The newest permit year is 2025. Treat a neighboring static level as a site clue, not a long-term groundwater trend.

Montezuma well drilling cost and the finished system

Local drilling is priced for fractured mountain bedrock, described in the cost model as granite and gneiss. A typical drilling rate is $65 to $100 per foot. At the recorded median depth of 74 feet, the drilled hole is estimated at about $4,800 to $7,400.

A finished system with pump, pressure tank, trenching, and electrical is estimated at $10,800 to $19,400 at that median depth. That is a planning range, not a fixed bid. A deeper hole, difficult access, extra casing or grout, and power or trench conditions can change the total. Request separate line items for drilling, casing, development, testing, pump equipment, trenching, electrical, and permits.

Risks to check before building on a Montezuma lot

Low yield is not the only concern. Fractured-bedrock wells can be unpredictable, and a dry hole or poor-producing interval is possible even where nearby wells work. The records also map 6 PFAS sampling or contamination sites, 680 historic mine features, and 12 EPA Superfund or cleanup sites within about 10 miles. Those mapped features do not prove contamination on a specific parcel, but they justify water testing and a site-specific environmental review.

Confirm the well permit and allowed use before relying on the supply. A household well may be exempt, but exempt wells still have limits; Colorado's exempt-well pumping limit is 35 gallons per minute. Check septic setbacks, replacement-area requirements, driveway access, slope, and building envelopes before placing a home. A well location that works on paper may conflict with septic or construction access.

A practical plan for buying or building near Montezuma

Start with the parcel's own permit, then compare nearby wells in Montezuma and surrounding Keystone, Dillon, Silver Plume, Breckenridge, Frisco, and Blue River. Look for depth, yield, static level, construction details, and the permitted use, while remembering that only part of the local record includes each measurement.

Before closing, make the purchase agreement address well inspection, flow testing, water-quality testing, permit verification, and a remedy if the system does not perform. For raw land, have a driller review access and likely drilling conditions before finalizing a house plan. Keep the well, septic field, driveway, and electrical route coordinated from the start; moving one later can add excavation and permitting complications.
