Calhan well depths vary widely across the Denver Basin

The median reported well depth near Calhan is 320 feet. The middle half runs from 107 to 500 feet, so a driller's bid based only on the median can miss the conditions on a specific lot. The 90th-percentile depth is 701 feet, and the deepest recorded well reaches 1,578 feet.

Depth mix gives a clearer picture: 23% of wells are under 100 feet, 23% are 100 to 300 feet, 37% are 300 to 600 feet, and 17% are deeper than 600 feet. Permits name Denver, Arapahoe, or unnamed fractured-bedrock aquifers. Denver Basin aquifer wells account for 100% of the listed aquifer records.

Most Calhan wells report useful production, but test the lot

Among wells with a reported pump yield, the median is 8.0 gallons per minute. The middle half produces 5.0 to 11.0 gallons per minute. That is a more useful planning range than assuming every nearby well makes the same amount.

The recorded yield mix is relatively favorable for household planning: 72% fall between 5 and 15 gallons per minute, 11% report more than 15 gallons per minute, and 16% fall between 1 and 5 gallons per minute. Only 1% report under 1 gallon per minute. These are reported well yields, not a promise of continuous supply. Ask for the well construction report and pumping information before relying on the number for a house, stock use, or irrigation.

Water levels near Calhan deserve a closer look

Reported static water levels have a median depth of 160 feet below the surface, with the middle half between 80 and 235 feet. A buyer should use that range when discussing pump setting, trenching, and electrical work, while recognizing that water level depends on the well, season, and measurement conditions.

The decade series shows a higher median measured depth to water in the 2020s than in the 2010s: 182 feet below surface compared with 152 feet. That points to a possible local decline in the available measurements, but only 75 wells report static water levels, so it is not enough by itself to establish a basin-wide trend. Have a qualified professional review nearby, comparable wells.

A Calhan well budget starts with depth

The local cost model assumes Denver Basin sedimentary bedrock and a drilling rate of $50 to $70 per foot. At the 320-foot median depth, the drilled hole models at about $16,000 to $22,400. A finished well, including the pump, tank, trenching, and electrical, models at about $22,000 to $34,400.

That range is a planning figure, not a fixed quote. The wide recorded depth range—from 107 to 500 feet for the middle half of wells—shows why depth can move the bid substantially. Ask what is included, how extra footage is priced, and whether pump equipment, casing, grout, water testing, trenching, and electrical are separate. Confirm the permit requirements before construction.

Check contamination, low yield, and the building site

There are 5 mapped PFAS sampling or contamination sites within about 10 miles of Calhan. The records show 0 historic mine features and 0 EPA Superfund or cleanup sites in that area. Those mapped results do not certify water quality at a property. Test an existing well, and plan for laboratory testing of a new well after construction.

Low yield, a dry hole, fractured-bedrock variability, and deeper-than-expected drilling remain site risks. Septic placement and the well location also need to work with the proposed house, driveway, slopes, and future repairs. A Colorado well permit controls the allowed use. Exempt household-use wells have a 35-gpm pumping limit, and an augmentation plan may be required in an over-appropriated basin.
