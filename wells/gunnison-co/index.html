Gunnison well depth: shallow records with mountain-bedrock uncertainty

The median reported well depth near Gunnison is 42 feet. The middle half of reported wells falls between 27 and 83 feet, and 77% are under 100 feet. Another 17% are between 100 and 300 feet, while 5% fall between 300 and 600 feet and 1% are deeper than 600 feet. The deepest record reaches 797 feet.

For a drilling bid, that spread matters more than the median alone. A shallow hole may be enough where groundwater is close to the surface, but fractured granite and gneiss can change conditions over a short distance. A contractor should review nearby completion records and discuss the possibility of extending the hole if the first target zone does not produce.

Reported Gunnison well yields are strong, but verify the test

Among the 507 wells that report a pump yield, the median is 15.0 gallons per minute. The middle half is also 15.0 to 15.0 gallons per minute, and 76% report more than 15 gallons per minute. Another 22% report between 5 and 15 gallons per minute, while 3% report between 1 and 5 gallons per minute. No reported wells are under 1 gallon per minute.

Those results are encouraging for household use, but they are not a promise for a new well. Yield records cover only part of the mapped wells and may reflect different testing methods. Ask for the actual well construction and pump-test documents, not just a listing claim. Storage tanks can help manage demand when production is intermittent.

Gunnison water levels look stable across the reported decades

The median static water level is 8 feet below the surface, with the middle half between 5 and 18 feet. By measurement decade, the median was 9 feet in the 2000s, 8 feet in the 2010s, and 8 feet in the 2020s. That series does not show a clear dropping water table in these records.

Only 120 wells report a static water level, so the trend is limited rather than a site-specific guarantee. A water level can vary with season, pumping, recharge, and local geology. During due diligence, compare the subject parcel with nearby wells and ask whether a recent measurement was taken while the well was resting or under use.

What a Gunnison well may cost to drill and finish

Near Gunnison, the cost model assumes fractured mountain bedrock, including granite and gneiss. The typical drilling rate is $65 to $100 per foot. At the 42-foot median depth, the drilled hole is estimated at about $2,700 to $4,200. A finished well, including pump, tank, trenching, and electrical work, is estimated at $8,700 to $16,200.

These are planning figures, not a fixed bid. A deeper hole, difficult access, extra casing or grout, hard drilling, or a low-producing zone can change the total. Ask each contractor to separate drilling from completion equipment and site work. Also confirm what happens if the first hole is dry or produces less water than the intended home requires.

Mine sites, septic planning, and low-yield risks to check

Most permits name an unnamed or fractured-bedrock aquifer: 2,637 wells. Alluvial aquifers appear on 41 permits, and Dakota appears on 3. There are no Denver Basin aquifer wells in the records. Mapped environmental sites within about 10 miles include 11 PFAS sampling or contamination sites, 219 historic mine features, and 6 EPA Superfund or cleanup sites.

These mapped features do not establish contamination on a particular parcel, but they justify testing and a careful location review. Check water quality before closing and keep the well separated from septic components as required by the applicable rules. Confirm setbacks, driveway access, winter construction conditions, utility routes, and available buildable ground before relying on a well permit.
