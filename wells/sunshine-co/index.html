Sunshine well depths reflect fractured mountain ground

The median permitted well depth around Sunshine is 290 feet. The middle half runs from 157 to 436 feet, which is the range a buyer should keep in mind when comparing drilling bids. A bid based only on the median can be misleading because the local ground is fractured mountain bedrock, including granite and gneiss.

Depths are spread broadly: 16% of wells are under 100 feet, while 37% are between 300 and 600 feet. The 90th percentile is 600 feet, and the deepest record reaches 4,550 feet. That extreme does not predict a typical lot, but it shows why a drilling contract should explain footage pricing, casing, grout, and what happens if the hole must continue.

Most Sunshine wells make usable household quantities

Among the wells reporting pump yield, the median is 7.0 gallons per minute, with the middle half between 5.0 and 10.0 gallons per minute. That is a generally workable range for ordinary household use when the well, pressure system, storage, and plumbing are properly designed.

The distribution still matters. 69% of reported yields fall between 5 and 15 gallons per minute, while 18% are between 1 and 5. Only 2% are under 1 gallon per minute, but a low-producing well can still affect building plans and daily use. Ask for the pumping test, recovery information, and permit conditions rather than relying on a seller's verbal estimate.

Water levels in Sunshine appear deeper in newer records

The reported static water level has a median depth of 70 feet, with the middle half between 30 and 100 feet below the surface. Static level means the water standing in the well when it is not being pumped; it is not the same as the bottom of the well or its sustainable yield.

The decade series shows a deeper recorded water level over time: the median was 34 feet in the 1960s, 40 in the 1970s, 55 in the 1980s, and 100 in both the 2010s and 2020s. That suggests a possible decline, but only 114 wells report static levels, so the series is not a complete groundwater monitoring network. Treat it as a warning to investigate, not proof of a uniform decline at every lot.

Sunshine has a long permit history and several drillers

Colorado DWR records contain 3,621 permitted wells within the local data area. Of those, 2,420 are recorded as constructed, 2,512 report total depth, and 966 report pump yield. The newest permit year is 2025, so the area is still seeing well activity rather than relying only on historic records.

Permit volume was highest in the 1970s, with 827 permits, followed by 568 in the 1990s and 541 in the 1980s. The most frequent names in the records are Ground Water Systems Inc (Wilson, Richard R.) with 846, Ingram Drilling Inc. (Ingram, Stephen E.) with 261, and Walton with 199. A permit record helps identify local experience, but confirm current licensing, availability, and the scope of any proposal.

A median-depth Sunshine well can cost about this much

For fractured granite and gneiss, the local drilling cost model uses a typical rate of $65 to $100 per foot. At the 290-foot median depth, the drilled hole works out to about $18,800 to $29,000. That is the hole itself, not the complete water system.

A finished well with pump, pressure tank, trenching, and electrical is modeled at about $24,800 to $41,000 at the median depth. Actual bids can move outside that range if the site requires more footage, difficult access, additional casing or grout, special electrical work, or a longer utility trench. Get the assumptions in writing, including mobilization, testing, abandonment terms, and whether the price changes if the hole is dry or produces less than expected.

Sunshine buyers need to check water quality and the building site

Water quantity is only part of the risk. Within about 10 miles of Sunshine, the mapped records include 80 PFAS sampling or contamination sites, 666 historic mine features, and 65 EPA Superfund or cleanup sites. These findings do not establish contamination in a particular well, but they justify laboratory testing and a review of nearby land uses, mine history, and drainage.

Most local permits are for domestic or household use, at 90% of the records. Stock use is 2%, irrigation is recorded at 0%, and monitoring is 3%. Confirm the actual permit rights before assuming lawn irrigation, livestock use, or a second residence is allowed. Also check well-to-septic separation, room for a replacement septic system, driveway access for a rig, slope stability, and the location of the proposed house.

What to do before buying or building near Sunshine

Start with the closest completed wells in Sunshine and nearby Crisman, Lazy Acres, Gold Hill, Mountain Meadows, Seven Hills, and Tall Timber. Compare their depths, reported yields, static levels, aquifer descriptions, and construction dates. Similar bedrock and topography are more useful than a distant record that happens to have a good yield.

Before closing, request the well permit and construction report, verify the permitted use, and arrange an independent water-quality test. If the parcel has no well, have a local driller review access, geology, and likely footage before you set a budget. For a new build, coordinate the well, septic system, electrical service, storage, and house location together. A DWR permit is necessary, but it does not guarantee water production or a particular water quality.
