Franktown Well Depth: Plan for a Deep Hole

Franktown wells have a median reported depth of 355 feet. The middle half runs from 281 to 478 feet, so a driller’s bid based only on a shallow estimate would be risky. About 54% of recorded wells fall between 300 and 600 feet, while 18% are deeper than 600 feet. The deepest well in the records reaches 2,956 feet.

Depth varies with the selected Denver Basin aquifer, the local sedimentary sequence and the amount of usable water encountered. Permits identify the Upper Dawson on 1,540 wells, unnamed or fractured-bedrock aquifers on 1,255, and the Lower Dawson on 643. Those labels help with context, but only a site-specific design and neighboring well review can set a reliable target.

What Franktown Wells Produce

Among the 1,881 wells reporting a pump yield, the median is 13.0 gallons per minute. The middle half is 11.0 to 15.0 gallons per minute. About 64% report between 5 and 15 gallons per minute, and 35% report more than 15 gallons per minute. No reported well in this dataset is under 1 gallon per minute, while 1% fall between 1 and 5.

That is a favorable local record, but a reported pumping rate is not the same as guaranteed household supply. Storage, recovery, pump sizing and simultaneous demand still matter. Confirm the well’s permitted use: 78% of local permits are domestic or household, with stock, irrigation and monitoring uses also represented. An exempt household-use well is subject to Colorado rules, including the 35-gpm pumping limit.

Franktown Water Levels and the Long View

Only 304 wells report a static water level, so this part of the record is thinner than the depth and yield data. The median depth to water is 150 feet, with the middle half from 101 to 264 feet below ground surface. That affects pump placement, electrical planning and the amount of casing and equipment below grade.

The decade series does not show a simple, continuing decline. Reported median water levels moved from 62 feet below surface in the 1960s to 156 in the 1970s, 204 in the 1980s and 215 in the 2000s, then returned to 160 in the 2010s and 140 in the 2020s. These are measurements from different wells and dates, not a controlled water-table study, so treat the pattern as context rather than a prediction.

Franktown Well Drilling Cost at Local Depths

The cost model for Franktown’s Denver Basin sedimentary bedrock uses a typical drilling rate of $50 to $70 per foot. At the local median depth of 355 feet, the drilled hole is estimated at about $17,800 to $24,800. That is only the hole, not the complete water system.

A finished well with pump, pressure tank, trenching and electrical is estimated at about $23,800 to $36,800 at that median depth. A deeper-than-median hole can move the price substantially, especially if the 600-foot range or more is needed. Get a written proposal that states assumed depth, casing and grout, pump equipment, access, trenching, electrical work and what happens if the hole must be deepened or produces poorly.

Franktown Risks Before You Build or Buy

Water quality deserves its own investigation. The mapped area within about 10 miles includes 30 PFAS sampling or contamination sites, 24 historic mine features and 3 EPA Superfund or cleanup sites. These mapped locations do not prove contamination at a particular well, but they are a reason to obtain a current laboratory test and review nearby land uses and available agency records.

Low yield and dry holes remain possible even where the local median is strong. Fractured-bedrock wells can be especially unpredictable. Check the well construction report, pump test, static level and maintenance history. Also verify the septic reserve area, separation from the well, driveway access, building envelope and grading. If the basin is over-appropriated, confirm the water right or augmentation-plan requirements before assuming a new domestic well can serve the proposed use.

Due Diligence for a Franktown Lot

Before closing, pull the DWR permit and construction records for the property and compare them with nearby wells. A strong comparison set can include The Pinery, Ponderosa Park, Castle Rock, Elizabeth, Parker and Castle Pines, but the closest wells with similar geology are the most useful. Look for depth, aquifer, casing, static level, yield and any notes about problems.

For a new build, have a driller and excavation contractor review access for the rig, the proposed well location, septic layout, utility trenching and final grades together. Budget from the local median only as a starting point. Confirm permitted use, test the water before relying on it, and make sure the home design works if the well is deeper or the equipment costs more than the initial allowance.
