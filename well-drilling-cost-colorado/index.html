How Much Does It Cost to Drill a Well in Colorado?

Short answer: most Colorado domestic wells run $15,000–$35,000 all-in, with drilling alone at roughly $50–$75 per foot. But the real number is driven almost entirely by one thing — how deep you have to go — and that changes dramatically from the Eastern Plains to the high country. Here is how the cost actually breaks down, and how to estimate yours before you drill.

The quick numbers

Budget about $50–$75 per foot for the drilled and cased hole (higher in hard mountain rock), then add $6,000–$12,000 for the pump, pressure tank, trenching, electrical and water test. A typical 250–350 ft Front Range or foothills well lands near $18,000–$30,000 finished. Deep Denver Basin and fractured-granite wells can run higher.

What actually drives the cost of a Colorado well

Contractors don't quote a well as one flat number — they quote a rate per foot plus a set of fixed components. Understanding the pieces is the only way to know whether a bid is fair and to estimate a lot you're considering buying.

  • Depth × price per foot — the largest and most variable cost. Depth depends entirely on local geology, so two lots a mile apart can differ by tens of thousands of dollars.
  • Rock type & drilling difficulty — loose alluvium and sandstone drill fast and cheap; fractured granite and hard bedrock drill slow and cost more per foot.
  • Casing & grouting — steel or PVC casing and the sanitary seal that protects the water. Usually bundled into the per-foot rate, but deeper or unstable holes need more casing.
  • Pump system — submersible pump, drop pipe, wire, pitless adapter, pressure tank and controls. Scales up with depth.
  • Trenching & water line — the buried line from the wellhead to the house, priced by distance and how hard the ground is to dig.
  • Permit & water testing — the state well permit and a post-drilling bacteria/nitrate test. Small dollars, but not optional.

Watch the depth assumption. Every quote hinges on an estimated depth. If a driller assumes 300 ft and hits water at 500 ft, the per-foot cost keeps running — that extra 200 ft can add $10,000–$15,000. Always ask what depth the bid assumes and what happens if the well goes deeper.

Cost per foot to drill in Colorado

The per-foot rate is the number drillers quote first, and it covers the borehole, casing and grout — not the pump or trenching. In most of Colorado it lands in a predictable band, but geology moves it:

In the Denver Basin (much of El Paso, Douglas and Elbert), wells tap the Dawson, Denver, Arapahoe and Laramie–Fox Hills aquifers — deep, layered sandstones that reliably hold water but require a long, expensive borehole. In mountain bedrock counties, water comes from fractures in the granite, so a deep well and a shallow well can sit side by side. That unpredictability is exactly why looking at real neighbor data beats guessing.

Permits, water rights & the costs people forget

Colorado is a prior-appropriation water state, which makes well permitting more than a formality. A few things can change your cost — or whether you can drill at all:

  • You need a permit before drilling. The Colorado Division of Water Resources issues well permits; a standard household or domestic permit fee is on the order of $100. Your driller usually files it.
  • Permit type limits your use. A "household use only" permit covers indoor use in one home; a "domestic" permit on a large enough parcel can add lawn, garden and livestock. Bigger uses need different (and pricier) permitting.
  • Augmentation may be required. In over-appropriated Front Range and plains areas, a new exempt well may not be allowed without a court-approved augmentation plan to replace depleted water — a real cost to factor in before you buy vacant land.
  • Low-yield wells need storage. Fractured-rock wells sometimes produce under 1–3 gallons per minute. The fix is a cistern and a booster system, which is an added but very manageable cost.
Buying vacant land? Confirm what kind of well permit the parcel qualifies for before you close. A lot that can't get a domestic well permit — or that needs augmentation — is a very different purchase than one that can.

Colorado well cost FAQ

How much does it cost to drill a well in Colorado?

A typical domestic well runs about $15,000–$35,000 finished. The drilled and cased hole is usually $10,000–$25,000, and the pump, tank, trenching, electrical and water test add roughly $5,000–$12,000. Drilling alone is about $50–$75 per foot in most of the state.

What is the cost per foot to drill a well in Colorado?

Most drillers quote $50–$75 per foot for a standard 6-inch domestic borehole with casing and grout. Soft plains ground can be $35–$55; hard fractured granite in the high country runs $65–$100+. The per-foot rate excludes the pump, pressure tank, electrical and trenching.

How much does it cost to drill a well per foot in Hartsel or Park County?

Hartsel and Park County sit on fractured granite, so both depth and yield are hard to predict. Wells commonly run 200–600+ feet and quotes trend toward the high end, roughly $60–$100 per foot. Because a neighbor a quarter mile away can hit water at a very different depth, checking actual permitted well depths near the specific parcel matters more here than almost anywhere in Colorado.

Does a well permit cost extra?

Yes, but it's a small part of the total. The Colorado Division of Water Resources charges roughly $100 for a standard household or domestic well permit, and some counties add an administrative fee. Your driller usually files it. The bigger question is whether your area allows a new exempt well and whether an augmentation plan is required.

Why do costs vary so much between counties?

Because depth — the biggest cost — depends on geology. Eastern Plains wells can be 100–250 ft; Denver Basin wells in El Paso, Douglas and Elbert often reach 300–600+ ft; and fractured mountain bedrock in Park, Teller and Chaffee is deep and unpredictable. Deeper wells also need bigger pumps and more pipe and wire, so the whole system scales with depth.

How much does the pump and pressure tank add?

A submersible pump package — pump, drop pipe, wire, pitless adapter, pressure tank, controls and install — typically adds $3,000–$8,000, more for deeper wells. Add a water line trench from well to house ($1,500–$6,000), an electrical hookup, and a bacteria/nitrate water test to finish the system.

How do I estimate my well cost before buying land?

Look up the depth of existing permitted wells near the parcel, multiply the typical neighbor depth by $50–$75 per foot for the hole, then add about $6,000–$12,000 for the pump, tank, trenching and electrical. If nearby wells average 300 ft, budget roughly $21,000–$34,000 all-in.
